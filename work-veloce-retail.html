118 outlets, one stock figure, zero sales lost to a dropped connection

Offline-first point of sale, central inventory and a loyalty programme rolled out across a national supermarket chain.

The challenge

Veloce ran 118 outlets on a POS that required a live connection. A network drop in an outlet meant queues formed and sales were written on paper, then keyed in later — sometimes wrongly, sometimes not at all.

Stock figures were reconciled weekly, which meant the online store routinely sold items that were not on any shelf. Promotions were configured outlet by outlet, so a national campaign took four days to apply and never applied consistently.

How we approached it

01

Pilot in the hardest outlets

We started in the five outlets with the worst connectivity rather than the easiest, so the offline model was proven against the real failure case.

02

Offline-first transaction ledger

Every sale writes locally first and syncs opportunistically, with server-authoritative reconciliation for price and stock changes made centrally.

03

Central pricing and promotions

One promotion engine pushing to every terminal, with scheduled activation and an audit of what each outlet actually applied.

04

Rollout at two outlets a day

A staged national rollout with a trained champion in each region and a 48-hour support window per outlet.

What we built

  • Offline-first POS on Android terminals with local ledger and sync
  • Central promotion engine with scheduled national activation
  • Real-time stock visibility shared with the online storefront
  • Loyalty programme with tiering, points and targeted offers
  • Shrinkage and category performance dashboards per outlet
  • Cashier training programme delivered in eight regional sessions
