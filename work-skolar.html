A learning platform that holds when 9,400 students sit the same exam

A rebuilt LMS with adaptive paths, low-bandwidth video and an assessment engine sized for nationwide simultaneous exams.

The challenge

Skolar’s platform fell over during its own admission test. Nine thousand students submitting within the same ten minutes took the system down for forty minutes, and the company refunded an entire cohort.

Completion rates sat at 19%. Students on mobile data abandoned lectures because the only video tier available was 720p, and the course structure gave no sense of progress or what to do next.

How we approached it

01

Rebuild the assessment path

The exam flow was separated from the rest of the platform, made stateless and load-tested to four times the expected peak before anything else was touched.

02

Video for the connection people have

Adaptive streaming down to a 240 kbps tier, with audio-only fallback and downloadable lecture notes for every session.

03

Adaptive learning paths

Diagnostic placement and prerequisite-aware sequencing, so a student always has one obvious next action rather than a wall of modules.

04

Instructor authoring

A rebuilt authoring experience tested with 14 instructors, cutting course setup from days to a single afternoon.

What we built

  • Stateless assessment service load-tested to 40,000 concurrent submissions
  • Adaptive video from 1080p down to a 240 kbps tier with audio-only fallback
  • Offline lecture and quiz access in the mobile app with sync on reconnect
  • Diagnostic placement and prerequisite-aware learning paths
  • Instructor authoring with question banks and reusable assessments
  • Cohort analytics showing where students actually drop out
Results

Measured against the baseline we agreed at kickoff.

9,400concurrent exam submissions, no degradation
3×course completion rate improvement
−64%video abandonment on mobile data
“We had been told our traffic problem needed bigger servers. It needed a different architecture. That distinction saved us a year of spending.”
