Twenty-two restaurants, one membership, and a kitchen that keeps up

A guest app, membership programme and kitchen display system connecting 22 outlets across a restaurant and resort group.

Location

Dhaka & Cox’s Bazar

The challenge

Bayleaf ran 22 outlets with three ordering channels that did not speak to each other. A guest who ate at their Gulshan restaurant every week was a stranger at the Cox’s Bazar resort, and the group had no idea who their best customers were.

Kitchens received delivery orders on a printer, dine-in orders on a terminal and phone orders on paper. During peak service the sequencing was whatever the expeditor could hold in their head.

How we approached it

01

One guest identity

A membership record spanning restaurants, the resort and delivery, with tier benefits that apply wherever the guest happens to be.

02

Kitchen display, properly designed

A single queue across every channel, sequenced by preparation time rather than arrival, tested through four peak services before rollout.

03

An app worth opening

Table booking, pre-ordering, membership status and a resort booking flow in one place, rather than four disconnected experiences.

04

Rollout by service period

Each outlet went live between lunch and dinner service, with a trainer present for the first three peak periods.

What we built

  • Unified membership across 22 restaurants and two resort properties
  • Kitchen display sequencing every channel into one prioritised queue
  • Table reservation with pre-ordering and deposit handling
  • Resort booking integrated with restaurant membership tiers
  • Guest spend analytics across outlets and channels
  • Campaign tooling targeting members by visit pattern
